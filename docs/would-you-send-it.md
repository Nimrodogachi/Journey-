*A three minute email review for ecommerce teams*

## Why we would hold both

The original needs obvious repairs. The reviewed example is cleaner, but it still does not establish that a real coffee customer should receive it.

**The first button cannot deliver the guide.** Its destination is only #. The original also has a blank preview, unfinished image and missing-name risk.

**The promise and next action need a human check.** The reviewed version talks about a coffee guide but links to a marketing portfolio. That destination is deliberate in this demonstration. It would not fulfil the same promise in a real coffee journey.

**The footer still asks for replacement.** The reviewed file contains an educational note instead of verified sender details. Its unsubscribe tag has not been demonstrated working in a real send.

**Recipient and platform evidence are missing.** Neither file proves the intended audience, timing, missing-data output, live links or inbox appearance.

## 3  Make the copy match the available content

**New editorial adaptation for this exercise**

**Subject** Three checks before your next cup

**Preview** Start with your usual brew method and change one thing at a time.

**Greeting** Hi there,

**Headline** Make your first cup your own

**Body** Start with your usual brew method.

1. Keep the water amount consistent.
2. Use the same coffee dose.
3. Keep the brew time steady.

Then change one thing next time as you learn what you enjoy.

**Next action** Try these steps on your next brew.

The advice is now inside the email, the three checks are visible, and the unrelated portfolio button is gone. A plain greeting avoids an untested name variable. These are editorial choices, not tested performance gains.

This new excerpt has not been run through the checker or a sending platform. A real campaign still needs its approved footer and the checklist below.

## Use this on your next email

Before release, ask the owner to attach evidence to each question. Write “not checked” when it is missing. Keep the exact reviewed version with the decision.

**1. Does the message deliver its promise?** Compare subject, preview, body and next action. If a link is used, open its actual destination and confirm the promised content or offer.

**2. Will the right people receive it?** Record the intended audience, timing and exclusions. Ask the platform owner to verify the relevant journey rules.

**3. Does it work with missing data?** Inspect the authorized test version with missing names or event fields, plus mobile and desktop views.

**4. Are the sender details and opt out ready?** Verify the actual sender, reply-to and required footer details. Record working unsubscribe evidence from an authorized test.

**5. Who owns the release decision?** Name the owner, unresolved issues and any accepted exceptions. A reviewed copy file alone is not that decision.
